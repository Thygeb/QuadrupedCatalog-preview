Notes

  • Unitree's own page presents G1-D in two configurations, Standard and Ultimate, differing mainly in the presence of a wheeled mobile base. The figures given are for the Ultimate configuration, the only one that can move under its own power; the Standard configuration is a fixed-height column with no base at all ('/' for mobility speed, drive type and chassis sensors) and 17 degrees of freedom against the Ultimate's 19.
  • G1-D itself is presented as a dual-arm data-collection and training platform ('End-to-End Platform for Humanoid Robot'), not a bipedal walking robot: two arms, a waist and a height-adjustable column mounted on a wheeled base, with no legs.
  • The Ultimate configuration's mobile base drives on a differential-drive chassis supporting 360-degree in-place rotation, carrying one LiDAR unit, two depth cameras, two physical-collision sensors and two low-obstacle-detection sensors.
  • Width and length are given separately for the two configurations and differ slightly (620x620mm for Standard, 525x570mm for Ultimate) because of the added base; the column height range itself (1260-1680mm) is the same for both.
  • The high-compute module is described as available in 'multiple brands and models... such as Orin NX 16GB (100TOPS)', not a single fixed part.
